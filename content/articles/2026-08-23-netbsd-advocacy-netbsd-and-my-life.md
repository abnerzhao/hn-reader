## easy

I used NetBSD for two years. It never broke. I work and live with this laptop. My team uses NetBSD for a big job. We support 4,800 users. NetBSD helps me at work and home. My life got easier with it. I wanted to share my story. I hope it helps others see why NetBSD works well. My daughter and I now have more time together. No more stressful server crashes. My team is happier too. Every update is smoother. NetBSD ran our network without problems. It saves money and time. No more late calls. I even took my family to Alton Towers again. NetBSD made that possible. It changed everything for me.

Before NetBSD, I used Windows servers.

## medium

Gary Rolland has used NetBSD for about two years on his laptop and has never experienced any problems. He uses this machine both at work and at home. He wanted to use NetBSD at his job as well, believing it could do much more. The main purpose of this message is to explain what NetBSD does for his life and how it has improved his personal situation.

He lives in the United Kingdom and works with three other network administrators at a large UK company. Their task is to keep servers running and perform maintenance. They work in shifts and may be called out at any time, day or night. Unfortunately, he cannot reveal the actual company name. He was given permission to share details about his NetBSD usage. His network is mission-critical; any downtime would cost the company a lot of money. There are currently 29 high-end servers running NetBSD 2.0.2, handling very heavy workloads.

These systems include MySQL databases, Apache web servers, Postfix for email, and Samba for file sharing. The system handles approximately 870 gigabytes of data each day and sends about 1,200 emails daily. During peak hours, the HTTP servers manage roughly 35 requests per minute.

Years ago, his previous Windows servers caused frequent failures. One memorable incident involved a trip to Alton Towers with his twelve-year-old daughter. On the night before the trip, he discovered that all the servers had crashed. When he arrived at work, his daughter was disappointed because the trip was cancelled. This experience led to repeated conflicts at home and work. Seeing the instability, he convinced his manager to allow him to test two servers on NetBSD. After spending nights installing and configuring the new systems, the NetBSD machines remained stable while the Windows ones failed repeatedly.

His success persuaded the entire team to adopt NetBSD fully. The network became reliable enough that team members could work from home using secure connections. As a result, Gary found more time for his family, particularly his daughter. The transition transformed his professional life and personal happiness.

The team continues to improve efficiency with NetBSD, and Gary remains grateful for the project's development. He encourages others to support the ongoing work.

## hard

Gary Rolland, whose professional responsibilities as a network administrator underpin a mission-critical infrastructure for a large UK-based enterprise, recounts his protracted engagement with NetBSD as a transformative pivot in both occupational and personal spheres. His organization, reliant on a 29-server constellation operating exclusively on NetBSD 2.0.2, oversees functionalities ranging from MySQL database administration to Apache-hosted services and Samba-enabled network file distribution for approximately 4,800 geographically dispersed users. Rolland’s narrative begins with the untenable instability exhibited by their legacy Windows-based systems—a regimen of perpetual micro-interruptions that jeopardized scheduled personal commitments, such as a planned family excursion to Alton Towers. The visceral distress precipitated by these failures extended beyond technical inconvenience, fostering relational strains at home and an existential urgency to ameliorate the precarious operational landscape.

The technological odyssey commenced when Rolland elected to procure two NetBSD servers for experimental deployment. His initial foray necessitated nocturnal dedication, culminating in a 4:00 AM culmination of kernel compilation and service configuration tailored to their exacting requirements via pkgsrc—a repository intrinsic to NetBSD’s ecosystem. The migration strategy was tactical: servers were abstained until Windows infrastructures succumbed to catastrophic failures, thereby substantiating NetBSD’s resilience through empirical contrast. The successful demonstration catalyzed a progressive rollout, wherein the erstwhile apologists among his team gradually embraced NetBSD’s command hierarchy. Rolland’s pedagogical efforts, which included disseminating printed manuals and hands-on troubleshooting sessions, metamorphosed skeptics into proficient administrators. This acclimation phase, though arduous, was undergirded by the fortuitous abundance of time liberated from perpetual Windows server crises, thereby catalyzing a cultural shift toward self-directed learning among staff members.

The architectural leap to NetBSD engendered quantifiable enhancements in system reliability. The server cluster now processes a mean daily data throughput exceeding 870 gigabytes, while Apache servers manage 35 concurrent requests per minute during peak operational windows—a testament to NetBSD’s capacity to sustain heavy computational loads. Email infrastructure, facilitated by Postfix, channels approximately 1,200 dispatches daily, occasionally burdened by unsolicited attachments whose voluminous nature (notably a 12-megabyte package) underscores the platform’s adaptability to disparate use cases. The web application stack, developed in PHP—a codebase Rolland characterizes as both 'heavy' and imperfectly optimized—relies on NetBSD’s robust throughput to maintain service continuity for internal and external clientele. These metrics, substantiated by empirical data aggregated post-migration, contrast sharply with the pre-netbsd era, where Windows servers exhibited a propensity for spontaneous reboot cycles, particularly during resource-intensive MySQL operations.

The quantitative improvements were paralleled by qualitative enhancements in work-life equilibrium. Rolland attests to a marked reduction in after-hours emergency interventions—a logistical proposition that once consumed weekends and sleep cycles. The erstwhile on-call exigencies, characterized by abrupt workforce mobilization during unconventional hours, have become historical anomalies. His team now exercises flexibility, utilizing secure shell (SSH) protocols to manage systems remotely from domestic environments on a rotational basis. This autonomy fosters a recalibration of priorities, wherein Rolland reports augmented familial engagement and diminished stress. The sonic evidence of this transformation emerged during a subsequent Alton Towers outing, where absence of professional exigencies allowed for unencumbered familial merriment—a contrast to prior instances where occupational crises had disrupted such aspirations.

The organizational ramifications of this transition are equally salient. The executive, initially skeptical of NetBSD’s applicability to their scale, acquiesced upon observing the platform’s capacity to absorb shocks without catalyzing cascading failures. This endorsement extended to a pragmatic abandonment of the window-dominated status quo, with Noodal’s advocates increasingly vocal in their endorsement of the BSD-based paradigm. Rolland’s colleagues, once novices in kernel configuration and port-based software installation, now articulate a burgeoning proficiency in leveraging NetBSD’s architectural principles. This epistemological evolution is emblematic of a broader trend, wherein the erstwhile lugubrious Windows environment is supplanted by a culture of exploratory competence—a shift Rolland attributes to the open-source ethos inherent to NetBSD, which democratizes system management through its transparency and collaborative repository.

Rolland’s encomium for NetBSD transcends mere technical appreciation, encompassing metaphysical gratification derived from communal contribution. His gratitude is directed toward the collective efforts underpinning the ecosystem, spanning individual contributions and collaborative development activities. This sentiment culminates in a plea for continued nurturing of the project, articulated through the dissemination of his testimonial across both personal and professional platforms. The narrative’s cadence, alternating between technical exposition and introspective reflection, mirrors the duality of Rolland’s experience—a synthesis of administrative pragmatism and personal enrichment. The closure, reiterating NetBSD’s role in salvaging both professional efficacy and domestic serenity, functions not merely as a testimonial but as an impassioned advocacy for the platform’s transformative potential.
