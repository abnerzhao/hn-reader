## medium

I’ve been testing gpt-5.6-luna for weeks, and it’s surprisingly capable, fast, and smart. It routinely hits around 100 tokens per second and zips through my codebase, emails, and knowledge base. The real revelation, though, is the cost. Running complex research threads or searching thousands of emails often totals just tens of cents. With GLM 5.3, we now have another strong option at the performance-price frontier. I’d been so focused on the most expensive, top-tier models for coding that I’d missed how much the smaller, faster models have improved.

This progress matters because token costs are the main reason we aren’t seeing more consumer AI companies. The old playbook for building a major consumer app was simple: create a compelling, cheap-to-run website, attract users virally, raise capital, scale, and build an ad marketplace. This worked for giants like Google and Facebook. But adding AI introduces a significant inference cost on every single request, dramatically increasing the capital needed. For example, building a personalized daily news site with a previous-generation model could cost around $1 per user. Charging a sustainable $30 a month becomes impossible.

However, with models like luna, where the average cost is about $0.10, the equation changes completely. The business world is where this gets even more interesting. My Segment co-founder, Peter, and I recently compared notes. Across his ventures, including Charm Industrial and Revoy, he categorizes his work into two types: “IQ 180” work, which requires brilliant, novel solutions, and “token spewer” work, which is about being ultra-responsive—handling calls, nudging people, and general blocking and tackling. Peter is exceptionally sharp, yet he estimates that 95% of his work falls into the second category.

I believe demand for “frontier-level” models will keep growing, especially for breakthroughs in engineering and science. But I also think the demand for “fast, cheap, and good-enough” models is about to explode. Most daily interactions—with coworkers, vendors, and customers—require responsiveness and follow-through, not necessarily a genius-level insight. This is where the majority of human effort in companies is spent. While building the infrastructure for these efficient models in business will require new tools for safety and permissions, I’m confident we’ll manage it. If you’re also working on making small models more useful, I’d love to hear from you.
